      <section id="lesson1" class="content-panel lesson-section mb-4">
        <span class="module-number">MODULE 01</span>
        <h2>UI vs UX</h2>
        <p><strong>UI (User Interface):</strong> Buttons, typography, colors, forms and visual layout.</p>
        <p><strong>UX (User Experience):</strong> How easy, useful and satisfying a product is to use.</p>
        <div class="example-box">
          <strong>Example:</strong> A shopping app has attractive product cards (UI),
          but if customers cannot find the checkout button, the experience (UX) is poor.
        </div>
      </section>

      <section id="lesson2" class="content-panel lesson-section mb-4">
        <span class="module-number">MODULE 02</span>
        <h2>User Research</h2>
        <p>Understand users before designing a solution.</p>
        <ul>
          <li>Identify the target audience.</li>
          <li>Ask users about their goals and problems.</li>
          <li>Observe how they currently complete tasks.</li>
          <li>Organize findings into user needs and priorities.</li>
        </ul>
        <p>For a learning website, students might need clear course categories, readable lessons and easy navigation.</p>
      </section>

      <section id="lesson3" class="content-panel lesson-section mb-4">
        <span class="module-number">MODULE 03</span>
        <h2>Wireframes</h2>
        <p>A wireframe is a simple layout plan created before detailed visual design.</p>
        <div class="wireframe-demo">
          <div class="wireframe-top">LOGO <span>MENU</span></div>
          <div class="wireframe-banner">PAGE HEADING</div>

          </div>
          <div class="wireframe-bottom">CALL TO ACTION</div>
        </div>
        <p class="mt-3">Wireframes help you decide where content belongs before spending time on colors and decoration.</p>
      </section>

      <section id="lesson4" class="content-panel lesson-section mb-4">
        <span class="module-number">MODULE 04</span>
        <h2>Visual Design and Accessibility</h2>
        <ul>
          <li>Use consistent colors and typography.</li>
          <li>Create visual hierarchy with headings and spacing.</li>
          <li>Maintain sufficient text/background contrast.</li>
          <li>Give images meaningful alternative text.</li>
          <li>Make buttons and links easy to recognize.</li>
          <li>Design for keyboard access and different screen sizes.</li>
        </ul>
      </section>
